Programs

Three gardens, one hive.

Children grow in three age bands, each with its own rhythm. Everything is designed to grow with your child, from first steps in the soil to their first real projects.

2–3

Seeds

A gentle start, often with a parent or caregiver nearby.

  • Sensory play with soil, water, herbs and flowers
  • Songs, rhymes and breathing games with Asal
  • Naming feelings with simple words and faces
  • Short, playful garden visits
4–5

Sprouts

Big questions and small gardens of their own.

  • A garden bed to plant, tend and harvest
  • How the body works: heartbeat, breath, bones and food
  • Stories, songs and games from around the world
  • Calm-down tools children can use on their own
6–7

Blossoms

Real projects that connect the garden to the wider world.

  • Season-long projects: seed to harvest to meal
  • Early reading, writing and numbers through living things
  • Earth, sky and the cosmos: why the seasons turn
  • Helping younger children: everyone serves the hive

Little Scientists · series one

The Water Series

Four 30-minute sessions, one a week, following a single drop of water on its journey: from the cloud, into a plant, onto a pond and back up to the sky. Asal flies along the whole way.

Week 1

Grow a Rain Cloud

A cloud is a sky-full of tiny drops. When it holds too many, it shares them with the earth.

Children build a foam cloud on a cup of water and drip blue “rain” into it, counting drops until the cloud gets too heavy and the rain falls through.

Try it at home: next time it rains, look up together. What do the clouds look like right before?

See the steps
Week 2

Seeds that Drink

A plant drinks from its feet to its leaves, one tiny sip at a time.

Celery stalks stand in colored water and slowly carry it up to their leaves. Under a magnifying glass, children find the plant’s tiny straws.

Try it at home: stand a white flower or celery stalk in colored water overnight and check the leaves in the morning.

See the steps
Week 3

Float or Sink

Some things ride on top of the water and some sink down to rest. Each one has its place.

After a short treasure walk, children guess and test what floats. A whole orange floats and a peeled one sinks; a ball of clay sinks until it becomes a boat.

Try it at home: at bath time, guess before each kitchen item goes in. Will it float or sink?

See the steps
Week 4

Where Puddles Go

Water never leaves us. The sun lifts it back to the sky, and one day it rains again.

Children trace a puddle with chalk and watch it shrink in the sun, make a cloud in a bag for the window, and see their own breath fog a spoon.

Try it at home: tape a bag with a little water to a sunny window and look for “rain” inside each day.
